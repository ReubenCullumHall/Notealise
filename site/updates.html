What’s new

Recent changes

A running list of what each update adds and fixes.

Latest29 September 2026·47 changes

Version 1.4.0

  1. 01

    Getting started is shorter: six screens instead of seven. The “That note is already a file” screen is gone. Its Show me the file link now sits under the box where you write your first note, and opens the folder with that note picked out. The folder screen says what it needs to in one line

  2. 02

    Bringing in notes you already have is now a choice between two big cards, Import notes or Start fresh. The import controls only appear if you ask for them, and the screen grows smoothly to make room

  3. 03

    Enter moves you on through the first-run screens, the same as pressing Continue, except while you’re typing in a box or on a button you reached with Tab. The cards and Continue press in when you click them or press Enter, and each new screen settles gently into place

  4. 04

    Picking a colour on the font screen shows it straight away, and asks whether it should colour just the text or tint the whole page

  5. 05

    A brand-new folder is no longer mistaken for one you’d set up before, which could skip most of the first-run screens and say “Pick up where you left off”

  6. 06

    Clicking + for any colour shows the colour picker instead of opening it out of sight

  7. 07

    Once you open the import controls, Continue is the one way on — the extra “Skip — I’m starting fresh” link under them is gone

  8. 08

    The screen where you write your first note fits a normal-size window on Windows, without a scrollbar; the writing box scrolls inside itself instead

  9. 09

    Learn more about exporting from Notion is a plain link again, not a boxed button — in the first-run screens and in Settings → Data → Import

  10. 10

    A space (or saved preset) that used the Narrow lined page look now shows Lined, instead of a plain page, now that Narrow lined has gone

  11. 11

    In split view, drag a column by the strip along its top to move it left or right — the other columns slide out of its way

  12. 12

    Maths has its own box, like Notion. Click any formula (or add one from the / menu or with Ctrl/Cmd+Shift+L) and a box opens under it: type the LaTeX, watch it draw on the line as you go, and press Enter or Done. Shift+Enter starts a new line in a formula that has a line to itself

  13. 13

    New in the / menu: Inline formula, for maths inside a sentence — type /inline and press Enter

  14. 14

    Maths and links no longer turn into their code when the cursor is right next to them, or while you drag a selection across them. Backspace just after a formula removes it in one go (undo brings it back); just after a link it removes one letter and keeps the link

  15. 15

    Closing a note in split view now shrinks it away like any other tab, and the rest of the split stays where it was in the tab bar. The × at the top of a split column now closes the note too — drag its tab out of the split to keep it open

  16. 16

    Toggle lists. Type > then a space at the start of a line, or pick Toggle list from the / menu, and click the arrow to hide or show everything inside it — headings, lists, tables, formulas, colour, even another toggle. Ctrl/Cmd+Enter on its title does the same, and it opens with a small bounce. (Typing > used to start a quote; Quote is still in the / menu)

  17. 17

    You can see what you’re typing again. A heading’s # shows on the line you’re on, and bold, italic, crossed-out and code show their marks while your cursor is in them — type a space after and they turn into the style. A dash stays a dash until you type the space, then becomes a bullet, and an empty heading says “Heading 1” (2, 3…) in pale grey until you type

  18. 18

    Page looks have an Intensity slider and can take your accent colour. In Settings → Look → Page (and Your collection), slide from faint to bold, or down to nothing, and switch on Match your accent colour to draw the lines or dots in it. Every look now draws about as strongly as the others at the same setting, and the preview cards show the space you’re editing

  19. 19

    Lined paper behaves like lined paper. Your words sit on the lines, every heading and list lands on a rule, and tables and formulas take whole lines so nothing below them drifts off. Lined, Grid, Fine grid and Graph stay even however long the note or the window is

  20. 20

    Tables and formulas look clean on a patterned page — the lines and dots stop at their edge instead of running through the words — and tables line up with your text instead of sticking out on both sides

  21. 21

    Split view has a clear line between the columns, in your accent colour, that’s easier to grab. The patterns stop the same distance from it on both sides, and a note in a narrow column no longer scrolls sideways

  22. 22

    The website has a What’s new page: the last three updates, one card each, with the first few changes showing. Tap a card and it opens over the page, which blurs behind it, with the full list. The empty About page is gone

  23. 23

    Space emoji can wear your accent colour. In Settings → Spaces, two new buttons beside Select choose full-colour emoji or one-colour emoji in your accent, for every space at once — on the switcher, the space tabs, the path above a note, links and search. The emoji picker now has every emoji instead of 80, and you can search it by name

  24. 24

    Dragging a note that’s open in a tab onto another space now takes the tab with it properly. Before, switching back to the space you dragged it from could show it stuck on its old spot and fail to open

  25. 25

    Table columns can be resized, like in Notion. Drag the line between two columns sideways to make a column narrower or wider, so a column with a little in it no longer leaves a big gap. A table you resize is only as wide as its columns; one you haven’t touched still fills the page. The widths are saved in the note, and Cmd/Ctrl+Z undoes a drag

  26. 26

    Shift+Enter starts a new line inside a table cell (Enter still finishes the cell), and the cell you’re typing in is outlined all the way round, with rounded corners, instead of showing a small box at the top of it

  27. 27

    The first welcome note now starts by telling you how to make a note — a small boxed line at the very top of “Start here”: click Note at the top of the sidebar, or press Cmd/Ctrl+N

  28. 28

    On a phone, the website’s top menu keeps its page links dead centre, and the social links fold into one circle wearing the Instagram logo. Tap it and the others drop down in a column, with a soft spring, and the circle turns into a cross to put them away

  29. 29

    Link straight to a heading or a paragraph. In the [[ list, folders and notes now have an arrow: go inside to pick a note, then one of its headings, and the list slides along as you go. To link a paragraph, list item or quote, right-click the six dots beside it, choose Copy link to block, and paste it into any note. Clicking a link opens the note at that spot and lights it up for a moment. Obsidian can follow these links too

  30. 30

    Right-click a link to point it somewhere else. The list opens on that note’s headings, or on its folder if you right-click the note’s name; Esc leaves the link as it was

  31. 31

    A link to a heading lands on exactly that heading, even when another heading in the note has the same words, and clicking the same link twice takes you there both times

  32. 32

    Links no longer take a colour or a highlight, or a spelling underline. Colouring a sentence with a link in it colours the words around the link

  33. 33

    Settings is easier to find your way round. Twelve pages are now six — General, Look, Spaces, Data, Help and Updates — with tabs where pages belong together. Theme, accent colour and fonts sit at the top of Look, with everything else folded away underneath and the rarely used ones under Advanced. What stays on screen as you scroll, and how folder colours pass down, are one box of ticks each. Replay the walkthrough and the test folder are under More at the foot of General

  34. 34

    Search Settings from the box at the top. It’s ready to type in as soon as Settings opens, and understands everyday words, American spellings and typos — “dark mode”, “color”, “backup”, “where are my notes”. Pick a result and Settings scrolls straight to that setting and lights it up for a moment. Searching “text size” tells you where zoom is

  35. 35

    On Windows, Ctrl+W only ever closes a tab. Holding it down, or pressing it with no note open, used to close the whole app

  36. 36

    Tabs, split columns, the bookmark and sidebar notes now open and close with a soft bounce — the same whichever way you open or close them.

  37. 37

    Split view keeps your arrangement: opening or closing other tabs never takes over or refills a column, and a split you step out of waits as one tab you can click back into (it survives a restart).

  38. 38

    Clicking a tab no longer closes the one you were on.

  39. 39

    Dragging a split’s tab onto the bookmark now moves it in.

  40. 40

    The search bar’s glow now fades in softly when you click into it, instead of snapping on.

  41. 41

    Settings opens more smoothly — it lands with a small settle and its contents fade in — and switching pages inside it fades too.

  42. 42

    Folders in the sidebar open and close smoothly, and your notes fade back in when you clear a search.

  43. 43

    Hiding the sidebar slides it away instead of squashing it. Its button then tucks out of the way — move your pointer to the left edge of the window to bring it back.

  44. 44

    Bold, italic, underline, strikethrough and code work like a word processor: a partly-styled selection gets styled all over, press again to take it off.

  45. 45

    With nothing selected, Bold (and the rest) applies to what you type next.

  46. 46

    Dragging across a link selects its text instead of opening it.

  47. 47

    Dividers (---) draw as a line.

20 September 2026·15 changes

Version 1.3.1

  1. 01

    The download page opens with the Notealise wordmark writing itself large in the middle of a plain screen — white, or black if your computer is set to dark — then shrinking into its usual place as the screen fades away, before the rest of the page comes in. On a slow connection the page no longer shows itself finished, blanks, and starts over; the wordmark video now loads first instead of last, and the pictures further down wait their turn. Once per visit, and links straight to a section skip it

  2. 02

    On a computer, Try it on the download page is only as tall as its own controls now. On a big screen it used to stretch to the full height, leaving a long stretch of empty paper under the note, and on a short laptop screen the Colour row was cut off with no way to scroll to it. It plays itself on a computer too, the way it does on a phone, until you click it

  3. 03

    The row of linked notes at the top of a note now scrolls sideways with an ordinary mouse wheel as well as a trackpad swipe. Before, a note with more links than fit had no way to reach the rest unless you had a trackpad or a sideways-scrolling mouse

  4. 04

    When renaming, moving or deleting a note doesn’t work, the reason now appears on the strip at the bottom of the window like every other message, and says only what went wrong: That name is too long — try something shorter, or a shallower folder. It used to stop the app behind a grey system box with the app’s own internal plumbing written in front of the sentence

  5. 05

    Undo stays in the note you are in. Pressing undo once too many in a note you had just opened used to fill it with the previous note’s text — and save that over it

  6. 06

    Typing can no longer go dead after dragging a note about. Dragging a note into a split, a folder or a tab sometimes left an invisible layer over the page: the cursor could not be put back in the words and nothing typed until the app was restarted

  7. 07

    Renaming a note updates every link to it, including a link you wrote earlier in the same sitting — those were quietly left pointing at the old name. The linking note’s line in the sidebar now says the new name too, instead of quoting the old one until you looked elsewhere

  8. 08

    Renaming from the sidebar asks for the name, not the filename. The box used to show “Groceries.md”, so typing a plain name over it left a file the app no longer listed and the note disappeared from the sidebar. A name with a character that cannot go in a filename now says which one: A name can’t contain / or ? — saved as “Meeting 9-10-”

  9. 09

    A link to a note whose name can’t be a filename now opens it. [[What next?]] makes a note called “What next-” and every click after that opens it — before, each click made another copy

  10. 10

    A link into a folder you haven’t made yet makes it, inside the space you are working in, instead of failing with the app’s internal error text

  11. 11

    The search box has a clear button beside its two filters, so a search can be put away and the notes brought back without reaching for the keyboard

  12. 12

    Choosing a note after typing [[ now finishes the link for you — the closing ]] is added and the link appears straight away

  13. 13

    A bookmark at the start of the tab strip keeps the notes you come back to most. Drag notes into it from the folder list, from a search result or straight off a tab, and they stay a click away instead of being found again every time. Folded it is a small bookmark and a count; click it and it opens with a bounce to show what’s inside. Click one to read it, drag one back out to the tabs, or drag it away to remove it. Each space keeps its own, with its own name — double click the bookmark to rename it — and it can be switched off per space under Settings → Spaces → Note extras

  14. 14

    Notes can be dragged straight into the top of the window. Drop one on a gap in the tab bar to open it there, on the middle of a tab to read the two side by side, or on the left or right of the page to open it in a column beside what you are reading. Dropping on an empty page opens it. Search results work the same way

  15. 15

    Pressing Enter after naming a note at the top of the page now puts the cursor in the note, so you can start writing straight away instead of reaching for the mouse

17 September 2026·14 changes

Version 1.3.0

  1. 01

    Smaller downloads. The Mac download is now built for one kind of Mac at a time instead of both at once, and packed tighter — about a third of its old size — and both installers leave out files the app never used

  2. 02

    The favourite star on a sidebar row stays where it is when you hover the row, so you can click it again to unfavourite. Hovering used to swap it for the row’s other buttons, which left nothing to click

  3. 03

    The Lined page look is an even ruled page now. Every line takes exactly one rule — a heading as much as a paragraph — and the words sit just above it, like writing on paper. Before, a heading knocked every line after it out of step with the rules. The rules are spaced to fit a large heading, so ordinary text gets more room between lines

  4. 04

    On a phone, Try it on the download page has all its controls again. A strip of tools runs along the bottom of the note — Typeface, Spacing, Theme, Colour, Accent — the way a photo editor lays them out, and the note stays in full view above it while you change things. Turned sideways, it lays out like the desktop: controls on the left, the note on the right

  5. 05

    On a phone or tablet, the end of the download page says Not available on your device above the two download buttons, rather than drawing a path to downloads that can’t run there. Computers still get the path

  6. 06

    The wordmark’s write-on at the top of the download page is a real ink recording now, not a generated one — the generated version could let a stroke on “l” or “e” pop in slightly ahead of the pen instead of drawing smoothly

  7. 07

    Macs with an Intel processor have their own download, so neither kind of Mac downloads the other’s half. Download for macOS gives your Mac the version made for it. When your browser doesn’t say which Mac you have — Safari never does — it asks first, in the page’s own light or dark look, with where to check in About This Mac, then fades through to the install steps as the download starts. Those steps name the version you got, with the other one a click away

  8. 08

    On a Mac, the app’s update check only ever offers the version made for that Mac

  9. 09

    The download page is rebuilt around a numbered walk-through — what it is, what it isn’t, picking your paper, moving in, a working demo, then the questions — instead of one long pitch. It follows whether your computer is set to light or dark, and each page of the site carries its own quiet highlighter colour

  10. 10

    01 What it is shows the same two notes side by side behind a slider you drag: a Journal space in roomy cream serif, and a Research space that is dark, tight and packed with one-line rows, a chart, a table and a checklist. Same app, two completely different rooms

  11. 11

    Try it on the download page can now change the paper too — Plain, Lined, Grid or Dots — and each space keeps its own, the way the real app does. On a phone it plays through those choices by itself until you touch it. The note is sized to the roomiest setting it can be put into, so it no longer grows and shrinks as you change things

  12. 12

    In dark, the site is properly black rather than very-dark-brown — the same black the app itself uses — and the highlighter behind the headlines sits behind the words as a deepened marker instead of a bright yellow bar under them. Every page colour keeps its own hue in dark

  13. 13

    Start scrolling during the page’s opening animation and the rest of the front page now sweeps up into place in order, rather than snapping in all at once

  14. 14

    The two install guides — the pages explaining the warning Windows and macOS show the first time you open Notealise — look like the rest of the site now. They follow your light or dark setting instead of always being white, and use the site’s own headline and label type rather than your computer’s default font
